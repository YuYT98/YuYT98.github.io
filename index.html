I earned my B.Sc. in Mathematics with a minor in Statistics from the University of British Columbia, where I am now a Ph.D. candidate in Bioinformatics, supervised by Prof. Sophia Frangou (Primary Supervisor) and Prof. Roger Tam (Co-Supervisor). I was a visiting Ph.D. researcher at Harvard Medical School and McLean Hospital (a Mass General Brigham affiliate), hosted by Dr. Dost Ongur.

My research focuses on AI for Science. I am particularly interested in how computational approaches can help us understand the complexity and individual variability of the human brain. Building on my computational background, I integrate neuroimaging, neuroscience, and psychiatry with AI, machine learning, and deep learning to study individualized brain variation. My work spans normative modeling, brain age prediction, brain atypicality analysis, and brain heterogeneity, with the broader goal of developing computational approaches that enable a more individualized understanding of the brain.
